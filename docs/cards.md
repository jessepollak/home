# Cards

Status: design of record for [#2079](https://github.com/jessepollak/home/issues/2079), October 6, 2026. Bridge + Stripe and Rain sit behind one card-program interface; Immersve remains for regions neither serves. Only the Bridge adapter and existing Immersve event ingress are implemented. Cards are not live. This page authorizes synthetic public-sandbox probes and Stripe test mode only, not production funding or live card spend.

## Customer model

A verified, eligible customer gets a virtual card that spends USDC directly from their Home smart account. The card balance and the cash balance are the same balance (Jesse, September 24, 2026).

- **Approval-based funding is the target.** Immersve documents approval-based funding on Base: the customer grants a standard USDC approval and spends from their existing wallet with no deposit transaction ([Immersve funding protocols](https://docs.immersve.com/guides/funding-protocols/)). USDC stays in the smart account until a purchase draws it, so Home shows one cash balance and the card's available spend is `min(wallet USDC, remaining allowance)`, subject to Immersve limits. The approval mechanics (funding type, approved contract, whether the draw happens at authorization or clearing, refund path) are not publicly documented yet (Q6).
- **The allowance is the card's spend limit.** The customer chooses a finite USDC allowance for the registered Bridge program spender, sees spender and purpose on review, and can raise, lower, or revoke it at any time. An Immersve allowance needs separate program-specific validation. This is the first standing allowance under the bounded-approval rule below.
- **Deposit-based funding is the fallback** if approval-based funding is unavailable for Home's program. It moves USDC into a separate card balance, which Home would count inside the cash total as a non-sendable component with its own provenance, plus the double-count guard. Its prepare gate is kept below.
- **Purchases** are Immersve payment events (`Holding`, `Cleared`, `Reversed`) projected into Activity. With approval-based funding the onchain draw is also an ERC-20 transfer from the customer's account, so Activity must join the payment event and the transfer into one row instead of showing both.

**Bounded-approval rule (replaces exact-approvals-only; Jesse, September 24, 2026).** Other Home Actions require an approval equal to a spend in the same Action. That blocks any recurring spender. The card allowance exception: every delegation of spend is bounded and purpose-bound. (1) A one-shot Action still uses an exact approval equal to its spend. (2) A standing ERC-20 allowance is allowed only for a spender registered to a product in server config (first: the card provider's program spender), for a finite customer-chosen amount no greater than a product maximum, shown on review with spender and purpose, and revocable through the same Action kind. (3) The same rule covers a Base Account spend permission if a future funding strategy uses one: registered delegate, token, cap, period, expiry, and revocation shown on review. (4) Max uint, unregistered spenders, and unbounded permissions stay rejected. Slice 2 implements this in `server/money-actions/issue.ts` and updates [architecture](architecture.md) and [actions](actions.md) in the same PR.

## Provider seam

`server/cards/program.ts` owns the `CardProgram` port: account/card reads, enrollment, issue, freeze, reveal, purchases, funding, redirect hosts and event sources. `bridge/program.ts` owns Bridge customer/endorsement/Persona KYC and Stripe cardholder/card operations, freeze markers and Issuing Elements grants. Region routing remains future work.

- **`CardEventSource`** (`server/cards/provider.ts`) verifies/normalizes signed observations into `{provider,mode,eventId,kind,externalIds,occurredAt}`. Signature adapters and Immersve ingress behavior are unchanged. Bridge owns Bridge PKI and Stripe HMAC sources; webhook enablement remains independent.
- **Shared orchestration** owns program selection, state reduction, ownership, locks, idempotency, redirect validation and purchase storage. Existing accounts keep their provider; disabled/unreadable programs mean unavailable, never silent re-enrollment. New accounts use `CARD_PROGRAM_DEFAULT=bridge|rain`, or the sole enabled program when unset. An ambiguous/unavailable default blocks enrollment.
- **Funding** is `allowance-pull` or `deposit`. Crypto-wallet journeys require valid Bridge allowance funding config as well as `BRIDGE_CARDS_ENABLED`; a malformed allowance maximum or retired-spender value disables the entire `crypto_wallet` card journey, including reads, freeze, reveal and purchase refresh. Configuration failures emit one `cards-config` server event per process with an error name and non-secret code, never credential values. Sandbox financial-account journeys use deposit, and spending discovery/allowance set never offer approval for them. The Bridge-shaped registry and Actions confirm/issue guards remain unchanged until the next delivery slice.
- **Immersve-specific (do not generalize):** SIWE sign-in and deploy-first enrollment, cardholder token rotation, the `<listenerUrl>/<topic>` JWKS-signed envelope, `Holding`/`Cleared`/`Reversed` states, Funds Storage config, and PAN-token URLs. Bridge instead uses Bridge customers with a `cards` endorsement and Persona-hosted KYC, Stripe Issuing for card operations, Stripe Elements for card-detail reveal, and two event sources (Stripe webhooks for cards and transactions, Bridge webhooks for customers and KYC) with different signatures.
- **Available to spend** is computed by the funding strategy, not assumed: for `allowance-pull` it is bounded by wallet USDC and remaining allowance, then by provider limits the adapter reports. Bridge may [pull an additional amount at authorization](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/cards/overview/noncustodial) based on merchant category and the overcapture upper bound, refunding any difference after settlement. Home still shows one cash balance; a Bridge wallet can temporarily dip below the displayed balance minus hold, reducing available-to-spend until settlement and refund observation.
- **Bridge pull and Activity:** Bridge's [onchain pull is asynchronous](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/cards/overview/webhooks); a Stripe authorization event may arrive before the onchain transaction is confirmed. Activity must keep the authorization pending until the pull is observed, rather than treating Stripe approval as funded settlement. Bridge refunds are also asynchronous and may be batched; reconcile their observed onchain movement with provider reads instead of assuming one refund transfer per authorization.

The event seam has Immersve JWKS, Bridge PKI, and Stripe HMAC adapters with shared offline conformance tests. Bridge mode pins `https://api.sandbox.bridge.xyz` or `https://api.bridge.xyz`, Base 8453 and Base USDC; `BRIDGE_ENABLED=1`, explicit mode, Bridge webhook public key, Stripe webhook signing secret, pinned `BRIDGE_STRIPE_API_VERSION` (date with optional release name), and checksummed nonzero/nonmax program spender are required for the event adapter. The configured spender remains a placeholder until Bridge assigns Home's specific issuer; the event adapter alone does not authorize funding or Action calldata. Stripe test-mode endpoint secrets differ from live-mode secrets and must not be reused. Bridge `kyc_link` events with a null `customer_id` cannot be associated with a customer through this port and are deliberately rejected; customer status/KYC changes with a customer ID are retained as invalidation signals.

**Local Base fork proof (2026-09-25, not CI):** `bun apps/web/scripts/bridge-allowance-fork.ts` forked Base at latest block 51792769 into local Anvil, deployed a Coinbase Smart Wallet v1.1 through factory `0xba5ed110eFDBa3D005bfC882d75358ACBbB85842`, locally seeded 20 USDC into its balance storage, and had the smart wallet execute `approve(30 USDC)` for the published Base Bridge address. An impersonated published contract address then called `USDC.transferFrom` directly: 10 USDC within cap succeeded (balance 10, allowance 20), 21 above remaining allowance reverted, 11 above balance reverted, and after the smart wallet's `approve(0)`, a 1 USDC pull reverted. **This fallback does not execute the Bridge contract's own pull logic or prove a Bridge program:** verified `IssuerFactory.transferToDestination` requires a program issuer ID and authorized debitor not assigned to Home. Home's actual program spender may differ. No transaction was sent outside the local fork; the Action implementation is documented in Bridge Slice 2d below and does not authorize a live approval.

**Stripe test-mode proof (2026-09-28, Stripe CLI forwarding to a local server, synthetic data only):** real signed deliveries of `issuing_cardholder.created`, `issuing_card.created|updated` and `issuing_authorization.created` were accepted and stored as allowlisted IDs; real `Stripe-Signature` headers carry `t`, `v1` and `v0` segments, and Home verifies `v1`. A wrong secret logged `WEBHOOK_REJECTED` and a mismatched API version logged `API_VERSION_MISMATCH`, with nothing stored. A virtual card requires an open Stripe financial account; freezing sets the card `inactive`, and a frozen card's authorization declined `card_inactive`. A card-scoped ephemeral key for Issuing Elements was issued server-side; browser reveal is unverified. Through test helpers, an authorization on the funded financial account was approved, captured into a transaction, and partially refunded, and a second authorization was reversed. Transaction webhook delivery to Home was not exercised live. Run `stripe listen` with an explicit `--events` list that excludes `issuing_authorization.request`: an all-events CLI listener becomes the account's real-time authorization endpoint and Stripe declines purchases with `webhook_error`. The Stripe sandbox program is a commercial program funded by a Stripe financial account; Bridge's consumer program funds purchases by pulling Base USDC, which this proof does not exercise.

**Fallback `card-fund` deposit gate (only if approval-based funding is unavailable; not implemented):** It is unavailable unless mode resolves to **production**, funding type is exactly `base-mainnet-usdc-universal-evm`, and token is the configured `BASE_USDC` on Base 8453. In addition it requires (a) a verified session with a smart account; (b) a Home record binding that owner to a completed Immersve login's `cardholderAccountId`; (c) a live `GET /api/funding-source/{id}` read proving a Funding Source whose depositor `fundingAddress` equals the session smart-account address case-insensitively and whose channel matches the configured ID/type; (d) a live funding-channel read and spending-prerequisites deposit target matching the configured channel, storage address, and mode — fail closed on missing access or mismatch; returned transaction params are compared, never executed; (e) Base `eth_getCode(storage) !== "0x"` at prepare; (f) config rejects both Funds Manager protocol addresses and the public Amoy storage and channel; (g) reuse the hashless-recent and in-flight guards from cash-out; (h) warn that Immersve spendable may be below balance because of limits, AML exclusions and pending withdrawals (formula unknown). Depositing before enrollment could strand funds until cardholder-session withdrawal, so enrollment is a hard prepare prerequisite.

Either funding path's first real transaction is **Base mainnet only**, small amount, after production program approval and Jesse's explicit authorization; neither sandbox validates a Home approval or deposit because Home signs only on Base 8453.

The sandbox simulator is out of Slice 1. Funding-wallet ERC-1271 support documented by Immersve does **not** establish Wallet Signature Funding Source creation or SIWE login acceptance for a counterfactual smart account.

The `card_events` record holds only verified notification identity, kind, occurrence time and allowlisted cardholder/card/transaction/customer IDs, never the raw body. Migration `019_card_events_provider.sql` retains existing Immersve records (provider defaults to `immersve`), renames `topic` to `kind`, and changes the key to `(provider, mode, event_id)`. Messages from unknown owners are expected in a shared environment. All rows, including unknown owners, become eligible for bounded lazy deletion after 30 days on later deliveries; inactive environments have no background purge or strict deletion deadline. Because a valid Immersve signature does not expire, a verified delivery is accepted only when its signed `createdAt` is a valid ISO timestamp newer than 30 days ago and no more than 5 minutes in the future. That replay window matches the dedupe retention even after old rows are pruned. Do not use these events as transaction or balance state: at request time, read the provider transaction or Funding Source afresh, with stale/unavailable provenance. Bridge's delivery timestamp is capped at 10 minutes and Stripe's at 5 minutes; validly signed stale deliveries receive retryable 400 responses because retries generate fresh timestamps and signatures, while stored event IDs retain the 30-day pruning rule. Bad signatures, malformed headers or bodies, and mismatched Stripe API versions remain deliberate 202 rejections; stale deliveries never enter the store.

**What Stripe test mode does and does not rehearse for Bridge** ([Bridge + Stripe Issuing](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/cards/overview/stripe-issuing), [funding strategies](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/cards/overview/funding-strategies)). Same: card operations after issuance use the Stripe API on Home's own Stripe account (`POST /v1/issuing/cards`, freeze via card status, spending controls, authorization and transaction objects, `issuing_*` webhooks, and Issuing Elements for card detail). Different:

- **Cardholder.** Bridge creates the Stripe cardholder after the Bridge customer's `cards` endorsement is approved and returns `stripe_cardholder_id`; the cardholder is read-only in Stripe and its status is Bridge-managed. The test proof created cardholders directly.
- **Card creation.** The card is created within 24 hours of the endorsement (otherwise the endorsement lapses and must be re-requested) and names its funding with `crypto_wallet` (`type=standard` for a noncustodial wallet), not `financial_account_v2`.
- **Funding and declines.** Bridge pulls Base USDC under the customer's allowance at authorization; the test program is a commercial program funded by a Stripe financial account, so balance and decline behavior differ.
- **Account linking.** Bridge connects to a Stripe account through a Bridge-supplied Stripe App install link and activates Issuing for the program; whether that is this test account or a new one is confirmed with Bridge.
- **Network.** Bridge documents Visa acceptance; the test program issued Mastercard.

Home therefore keeps a Bridge client for customers, endorsements, and wallet linkage, and a Stripe client for card operations; only the Stripe half is exercisable before Bridge sandbox access.

## Card program schema and contract

`022_card_programs.sql` backfills sandbox-only rows, then replaces the Bridge/Stripe columns without shims or dual-writes. Accounts have `provider`, `provider_account_id`, `provider_cardholder_id`; `(customer_id,mode)` remains the primary key, with unique `(customer_id,mode,provider)` and partial unique `(provider,mode,provider_account_id|provider_cardholder_id)` indexes. Cards retain their Home UUID and wallet, gain `provider` and `provider_card_id`, and use unique `(provider,mode,provider_card_id)` plus a cascading account FK `(customer_id,mode,provider)`. Provider checks include Bridge, Rain and Immersve.

Purchase joins match provider, mode and provider-card ID. `card_events_provider_card_idx(provider,mode,card_id,received_at) WHERE card_id IS NOT NULL` serves selective pending-event reads. The transactional migration takes table locks and builds the index normally: appropriate for sandbox-only data; a live/growing deployment needs a separately reviewed rollout. Store tests use committed migrations through `tests/helpers/migrations.ts`.

`GET /api/cards` contract **v2** returns private/no-store Home UUID card IDs, ten states, and `{program,account,cards,fetchedAt}` provenance; provider IDs never appear in the list. Enrollment returns `{version:2,next:{kind:"redirect",url}|{kind:"complete"}}`. Server validation requires an exact HTTPS host in the program's allowlist; the public parser accepts only `bridge.withpersona.com` today, without credentials, fragments or nonstandard ports. Complete enrollment refreshes in place.

### Ordered card-state reducer

First-match-wins over a stored link, fresh program account `A` and fresh linked card reads `C`. `server/cards/journey.test.ts` names each row; `bridge/program.test.ts` covers Bridge customer, endorsement and cardholder mappings.

### Writes, races and owner boundaries

| Transition | Failure/timeout/partial | Concurrent request | Owner change | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enrollment → verification/ready | unavailable; account and cardholder IDs commit before requesting or validating the KYC link; retry reuses the stored account | account row lock during enrollment; one sticky account; KYC link request outside the transaction | session customer scope, late client result fenced | integration enrollment / failed-link retry tests |

Create keys derive from SHA-256 of `[provider,purpose,mode,customerId,...]`; issue includes wallet and prior-card count for replacement keys. Freeze/unfreeze uses a fresh random request key after fresh-state gating, not a replayable create key. The provider-agnostic wallet lock is `cards:${mode}:${wallet}`. Another customer's card on that wallet is read through its own program: unavailable/disabled/unreadable means `CARDS_UNAVAILABLE`, non-canceled means `CARD_CONFLICT`. `write-service.integration.test.ts` proves the unavailable-program conflict fails closed.

`POST /api/cards/{UUID}/reveal` replaces the deleted ephemeral-key route. Stripe needs an issuing-card ID before creating its nonce, so one route supports two explicit steps: `{method:"stripe-issuing-elements",step:"prepare"}` returns `{version:2,cardId,grant:{method,step,issuingCard}}`; `{method,step:"grant",nonce}` returns the matching issuing-card ID, nonce and ephemeral-key secret in `grant`. Both steps require session auth, same-origin JSON, owned UUID and fresh active/frozen card and account state. Provider IDs leave the server only in authorized reveal responses. No fresh wallet signature is required; no secret or PAN/CVC is logged/persisted.

### Bridge Slice 2b UI: the Card tab

`Card` is a third primary navigation tab at `/card`, between Home and Invest, rendered only when the server reads `BRIDGE_CARDS_ENABLED=1`; the page passes only that boolean to the client. When disabled the tab is absent and `/card` redirects to `/home`. The screen reads `GET /api/cards` through `client/cards/use-cards.ts` and the shared parsers, and renders every `CardState`: not enrolled (`Get your card`) and verification required (`Verify`) both call `POST /api/cards/enrollment` and navigate to the returned Bridge-hosted link; verification pending shows progress with no action; ready to issue (`Create your card`) and canceled (`Get a new card`) call `POST /api/cards`; ineligible shows a notice; active and frozen show every non-canceled card newest first, each with its art (last four only), `Card details` and `Lock card` switch (`POST /api/cards/{id}/freeze|unfreeze`); restricted shows an on-hold notice, keeps freeze available for an active card and disables unlock, and with no issued card shows only the notice with no action; unavailable or a failed read offers `Try again`. After every write the state is re-read rather than inferred: the write reports success only when a completed re-read (not paused offline) returns a state other than unavailable and shows the intended result (the target card frozen after a lock, active after an unlock, the issued card live after a create); otherwise it shows `Couldn't refresh your card. Try again.` When the latest read fails the screen shows the unavailable state instead of earlier card data. A write that settles after the signed-in account changes shows nothing. Cards that share their last four digits add their position to accessible labels (`Card 2 ending 4821`).

Card details load Stripe.js on demand with `NEXT_PUBLIC_STRIPE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`, prepare reveal, create a nonce for that `issuingCard`, request the matching grant, and mount number/expiry/CVC Elements. Unset/malformed keys hide the control; restricted/canceled cards cannot reveal. PAN/CVC stay inside Stripe iframes, never Home's DOM/state/server. Real Issuing Elements remains unverified without a provisioned test card.

## Bridge Slice 2c: card purchases in Activity

`card_transactions` is an owner-scoped projection unique on `(provider,mode,provider_transaction_id)`, storing validated program purchases without PAN/CVC or raw bodies. `program.purchases.refFor` selects detail refreshes; reads/lists belong to the adapter. Activity refresh is bounded to two cards, ten oldest pending references per card (eleven-row sentinel), three 25-item pages per Stripe authorization/transaction list and 25 writes per card. Partial lists, provider failures, mismatches and budgets mark unavailable while retaining persisted rows. Each response is limited to 64 KiB. Activity purchase IDs are Home `card_transactions.id` UUIDs, not provider transaction IDs; the contract version and shape otherwise stay unchanged.

Activity's first page includes card rows within the same 31-day window alongside onchain transfers and Home actions, keyed by Home purchase UUID and scoped through `cards.customer_id` (no extra card rows on pagination). A transaction replaces its linked authorization in the feed, while a refund never supersedes a completed capture: a full or partial refund appears as a separate `refunded` row alongside the original `completed` capture and its original amount. An unlinked approved authorization remains pending unless Stripe explicitly reverses it; Stripe closure alone does not prove settlement. `pending` is an approved authorization or provider-pending transaction; `declined` uses Stripe's last request reason when supplied; posted captures are `completed`, voided transactions and reversed authorizations are `reversed`, and posted refunds are `refunded`. Pending card purchases do not assert that Bridge's asynchronous wallet pull cleared. Provider failure returns explicit `cards.status=unavailable`, never an authoritative empty-feed claim. Onchain failure returns a partial response only when card rows exist; otherwise it returns the original onchain error code and status. The client retries transient first-page onchain failures and partial responses up to twice (after 0.5 s and 1.5 s); failed or partial revalidation keeps previously loaded rows, and a partial revalidation of the same window replaces card rows with its fresh ones even when the last retry fails after an earlier partial. A newer-window partial refresh replaces the loaded card snapshot wholesale without advancing the transfer window or showing a latest-activity warning; pull-to-refresh still reports that transfers did not refresh. The owner-scoped, in-memory snapshot survives older-window query commits until the transfer window catches up, keeping its row IDs and order while using a current-page copy only when its `updatedAt` is later; a ready, uncapped first-page card read started after the snapshot's card read finished also reconciles it: current-only rows inside the snapshot window are added, and rows it no longer returns within its own window are removed, while newer-only rows remain. Pagination and older in-flight reads do not authorize removal; a deep-equal newer read keeps the cached card object but advances its read time, and restored cache data without an in-memory read time does not authorize removal. Clearing owner query memory fences the snapshot from a later sign-in. Either snapshot or current-page card-source unavailability still shows the card warning. Reload starts a new window. Current card rows are a Stripe purchase view; joining onchain funding pulls and partial refunds into one accounting row remains dependent on Bridge's pull evidence and liability terms described above.

| Stripe purchase state | Activity treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Approved authorization without linked transaction | `pending`, or `declined`/`reversed` when Stripe explicitly reports it |
| Linked transaction | Supersedes only the authorization; a posted capture stays `completed` at its original amount |
| Posted refund, including a partial refund | Separate `refunded` row at the refund amount; never hides or rewrites the capture |
| Amount shown | Refund as money returned (`+`); pending and completed as money spent (`−`); declined unsigned with "Nothing was charged."; reversed unsigned with "Your balance didn't change." |

## Bridge Slice 2d: card allowance Action

`card-allowance` sets or revokes the Base USDC allowance from the verified smart account to a registered Bridge program spender. Prepare accepts `{version:1,operation:"set",allowanceBaseUnits:"<integer>"}` or `{version:1,operation:"revoke",spender:"0x…"}`. A set changes the current program spender's allowance to a positive amount no greater than `BRIDGE_CARD_ALLOWANCE_MAX_USDC` (whole USDC, at most 1,000,000); a revoke approves zero to the current or an explicitly registered retired spender. `BRIDGE_PROGRAM_RETIRED_SPENDERS` is an optional comma-separated list of up to eight checksummed addresses; more than eight, duplicates, invalid addresses, or the current spender fail closed. Move a compromised or replaced spender to this retired list, never simply delete it: customers must retain the ability to discover and revoke its permission. The registry comes from Bridge webhook config. A missing maximum disables setting, not revocation; invalid config disables the Action. Disabling Bridge (`BRIDGE_ENABLED`) or invalid Bridge config also disables in-app revocation and discovery; during an incident retire the spender instead of disabling Bridge. Revocation never requires an eligible card or an enabled card journey.

| Operation | Required prepare gates | Confirm and issue-time gates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set | Bridge webhook and card journey enabled in matching production mode; `crypto_wallet` funding; configured maximum; valid finite positive amount; verified smart account; existing owner-linked Home customer (no creation); locally linked non-canceled card for that wallet/mode; fresh `active` or `frozen` Bridge/Stripe state | Issuance revalidates current spender and configured maximum, exact server-encoded USDC call, optional paymaster prefix only. Before confirming an unconfirmed set or retrying a confirmed set after rejection, the program spender and set configuration must still match; the owner-linked customer, local wallet/mode card link, and fresh active/frozen Bridge/Stripe eligibility are rechecked. Revoked eligibility refuses confirmation or retry; unreadable state fails closed. |
| Revoke | Verified smart account; readable Bridge spender registry; spender currently registered or explicitly retired. Any mode and disabled card journey are allowed. | Exactly one USDC `approve(spender,0)` after an optional paymaster prefix; no zero-then-increase requirement and no set gate at confirm or retry. |

Eligibility uses the customer's overall fresh card state, not only the linked wallet's card. A restricted card on another wallet blocks a new allowance (fail-closed; declined S-03).

Prepare reads `allowance(owner,spender)` at one pinned Base block with a five-second deadline. An unreadable allowance never defaults to zero. Metadata records the block, previous and requested allowances, spender and maximum (null for revoke); `amounts: []` because permission changes spend no USDC. Review warns about the spender and purpose, and network fees follow the usual Action flow. Errors: `CARD_ALLOWANCE_INVALID` (400; invalid params, unregistered spender or amount outside cap), `CARD_ALLOWANCE_NOT_READY` (409; no eligible card), `CARD_ALLOWANCE_UNCHANGED` (409; onchain allowance already equals the request), `CARD_ALLOWANCE_UNAVAILABLE` (503; settings, provider state or allowance read unavailable). Other Action kinds cannot grant an approval to any syntactically valid current or retired spender from the raw environment, even when Bridge is disabled or the registry is malformed; unrelated approvals remain issuable. Card-allowance Actions still require the strict registry.

`GET /api/cards/spending` authenticates the wallet session and returns private, uncached version 1 data without creating a customer or reading card state. `available` contains `setEnabled` (the production journey/maximum set gate), the current registered lowercase spender, decimal-integer `walletBaseUnits`, `allowanceBaseUnits`, `availableBaseUnits` (their minimum), `retired` (all configured retired spenders with their decimal allowances, including zero), `blockNumber` and `fetchedAt`. Retired allowances are read at the same pinned Base block with the balance and current allowance under one shared five-second deadline, but never count toward `availableBaseUnits`; any failed or malformed read returns `unavailable` with `fetchedAt`, never a zero balance. Readable registries still return `available` when setting is disabled (maximum unset, journey disabled, misconfigured or sandbox), with `setEnabled:false`, except that deposit-funded programs return `not-configured` before chain reads. Deposit programs therefore show no current or retired revoke rows, while prepare-revoke remains available through the registered-spender gates above. A null registry (Bridge disabled) also returns `not-configured`; invalid registry configuration returns `unavailable`. A verified session without a smart account gets HTTP 503 `{version:1,error:{code:"CARDS_UNAVAILABLE"}}`; unauthenticated requests receive the same unversioned auth-boundary HTTP 401 `{error:{code:"UNAUTHENTICATED",message:"A valid access token is required."}}` as sibling card routes, not a card-spending error. A Bridge authorization before its asynchronous onchain pull is observed is not subtracted, so available to spend can briefly overstate spendable funds until the pull lands. Home's cash total is unchanged: the allowance limits the same wallet balance, never adds a second balance.

The Card tab shows one Spending section only when the ready card response contains an actual issued card in `cards` (including canceled history), with loading, unavailable/retry, not-configured and ready spending states; unknown spending never appears as zero. Pre-issuance states, ineligible and even active, frozen or restricted labels without cards do not show Spending, regardless of nonzero current or retired allowances. Setting or changing a limit stays disabled until `setEnabled` and an active or frozen card state permit it. Customers enter a positive USDC limit, review the server-prepared spender, purpose, current/new limits, Base network and expiry, and confirm through the wallet Action flow. Current permissions can be turned off even when setting is disabled, and nonzero retired-program permissions can be removed individually. The spending read refreshes after submission closes and when its outcome settles.

With canceled issued-card history but no live card, the Card tab still shows a revoke-only Spending section when ready data contains a nonzero current or retired allowance. It exposes only Turn off and nonzero Old card program / Remove rows; zero or unknown allowances hide the section. A stale-session execution stays on confirmation with “Your account changed. Sign in again and try again.” and a disabled primary control, without marking the action submitted or offering a same-action retry.

An open spending sheet survives spending refetches that return zero allowances or unavailable data, and card reads that lose their issued cards or become loading, failed or unavailable. Those reads may hide the Spending section without dismissing the review, pending execution or result. Only explicit close or an owner boundary change ends the sheet; owner changes still fence late wallet results and refreshes.

This code does **not** authorize a live approval: no production Bridge program or Jesse authorization is available, and the configured spender remains a placeholder. No mainnet approval or funded card spend is permitted until a production program is assigned and Jesse explicitly authorizes validation.

## Decisions before Slice 2

**Approved by Jesse, September 28, 2026. Decision 1 is implemented by Slice 2c, and both the re-approval/revocation mechanics and retired-allowance discovery in decision 3 by Slice 2d; the remaining program and policy decisions are not implemented yet:**

1. Approved and implemented for Bridge in Slice 2c: keep a durable `card_transactions` projection keyed by `(provider, mode, provider_transaction_id)`, written only from fresh provider reads, not webhook payloads. It survives the 30-day `card_events` prune and serves Activity; accounting and dispute evidence beyond the stored fields remain future work.
2. Obtain each provider's program terms in writing from Bridge and Immersve before production to determine liability for an approved authorization whose wallet pull later fails; Stripe approval alone does not settle this liability.
3. When a program spender changes, require the customer to re-approve via the `card-allowance` Action; never automatically migrate an allowance. Remove a compromised or deregistered spender as the current `BRIDGE_PROGRAM_SPENDER` and add it to `BRIDGE_PROGRAM_RETIRED_SPENDERS`, which blocks new approvals while keeping discovery and revocation available so customers can be prompted to revoke existing approvals.
4. Route disputes and chargebacks to support per [#813](https://github.com/jessepollak/home/issues/813) until the provider dispute API is integrated.
5. On a region move or program end, freeze the card and re-enroll the customer with the available provider; eligibility, remaining holds, and transfer of dispute evidence remain open.
