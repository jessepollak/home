# Leave Home

Leave Home deletes Home-held account identity and nonfinancial records. It does not move funds, submit transactions, close provider accounts, or ask providers to delete records. Public Base history cannot be erased. Providers are identified in the receipt as **held by <provider>, not deleted by Home**. Active provider cards remain provider-held.

## Authenticated boundary

`POST /api/account/deletion` requests deletion and attempts completion. Its body must be absent, empty, or `{}`. `GET /api/account/deletion` reads the receipt and retries a queued request. The owner comes only from the cryptographically verified session, never a request body, query parameter, or customer identifier. Both methods remain behind the existing deployment-access gate, are private/no-store, and use `shared/account/contracts/account-deletion.ts` (version 1, schema `home.account-deletion`). No public or machine access exemption is added.

POSTs converge on the partial unique queued-customer index. GET returns `404 ACCOUNT_DELETION_NOT_FOUND` without a request, including a verified session with neither a live credential nor a recovery tombstone. Conflicting/merged linkage returns `409 ACCOUNT_DELETION_LINKAGE`. Missing database, missing HMAC key, query failure, or abort returns `503 ACCOUNT_DELETION_UNAVAILABLE`. Logs contain generic route/outcome codes, not subjects, addresses, email, customer IDs, or raw failures.

Every class in `ACCOUNT_EXPORT_HOME_CLASSES` receives a disposition, in export order. Queued receipts say `pending`. Completed receipts are frozen and describe deleted stores, pseudonymized financial stores, provider-held records, and the immutable public-chain boundary. Provider labels are determined before deleting credentials and provider links. A completed receipt never contains a retained owner ID.

## Lifecycle and eligibility

Completion runs in one transaction. It resolves the request's live customer without a row lock, takes the deletion-only customer advisory lock first, then locks the request, takes sorted credential-digest advisory locks shared with customer creation, rechecks scope through `resolveExportOwner`, takes sorted legacy-address advisory locks shared with wallet enrollment, and locks the customer's card accounts and cards. Before classifying, it also locks every scoped action (including drafts and declined actions) and funding order, then every scoped cash-out order with `FOR UPDATE`, holding those locks through completion or the blocked attempt. Existing-action updates either commit first and are classified from their new state, or wait and fail their owner-key guard after pseudonymization; this is an attempt-local eligibility fence, not serialization of independent new actions while deletion is queued. It uses the existing action status classifier and funding store open-order SQL. Live unconfirmed prepared actions, unresolved outcomes, dispatch-ambiguous funding, unsettled/nonterminal cash-out orders, and pending card transactions or unreconciled issuing events block completion. An authorization with explicit `authorization_closed=true` is terminal regardless of capture amount or whether a capture exists: Stripe `closed` and `expired` both record closure. Open or unreadable authorization evidence remains blocking; missing closed-state evidence on older rows requires a refresh. An issuing event without a projected transaction, or newer than its last refresh, is unresolved even if a provider refresh failed. Unreconciled card events refresh only during card reads, not deletion GET/POST or sweep; a queued request may wait until a card read successfully reconciles the event. The deletion blocker shares the transaction store’s pending-event predicate; financial event pruning also requires terminal reconciliation. A queued deletion request is not a cross-action lock: new independent actions remain allowed. Unconfirmed drafts without any handle, submission timestamp, transaction hash or outcome stop blocking when their review expires (the same `actionReviewExpired` predicate used by confirm’s 410 response), or strictly after `ACTION_DRAFT_RETENTION_MS` (the same one-hour retention constant used by action-list pruning). This check happens inside the completion transaction and covers customer and legacy-owner scope; confirmed or evidence-bearing actions remain governed by the existing status classifier. Explicitly declined actions without a provider handle, submission timestamp, transaction hash or outcome are terminal only at or after their review expiry; draft age alone cannot retire a confirmed declined action. Activity’s shared non-declined predicate still scopes undispatched cash-out evidence. Their undispatched cash-out orders are terminal only without a proven deposit or deposit ID. A `not_submitted` cash-out without that deposit evidence is also terminal; either deposit evidence keeps the order blocking until reconciliation. Cash-out terminal states are `delivered`, `returned` and `failed`, verified against `planCashoutRefresh` in `server/funding/cash-out-progress.ts`; durable `settled_at` is also required. The regression `cashout state %s requires both durable settlement and a state terminal in planCashoutRefresh` covers terminal and ambiguous states.

Eligible completion creates a **new**, randomly identified, closed customer P. P has no credentials, wallets, country or invitation identity. First/last seen equal completion time and first-seen source is the neutral allowed value `backfill`. Financial rows are moved to P, credential/wallet links are removed, owner keys/subjects become `retained:<P>`, identity fields are cleared, and C is deleted. Live-session GET/POST resolves the customer from its credential without locking, takes the deletion-only customer advisory lock before an existing queued request and the credential advisory, rechecks scope under those locks, and unions the session's action owner key before releasing them. It saves the session's action owner key in nullable `session_owner_keys` on the queued request, unioning keys from successive sessions. Completion also scopes legacy null-customer actions across **every credential of C**, independent of recorded wallets or session publication: canonical `actionOwnerKey` JSON starts with `["<subject>","0x` and ends with `,8453,"<accountProvider>"]`. Credential subjects must fully match `^[a-zA-Z0-9-]{1,100}$`; an invalid subject fails closed with `ACCOUNT_DELETION_LINKAGE`. LIKE literal segments are escaped defensively and patterns are bound parameters. Exact session/wallet owner keys remain in scope. The same credential-wide scope applies to action locks, blockers, cash-out subqueries and pseudonymization; matching owner keys on a different non-null customer fail closed. Other subjects and the same subject under another account provider are excluded. Sweep uses this scope even if a linked credential has never published a session. These are queued-only identity, never exposed in receipts and cleared to null atomically at completion. The deletion request's `customer_id` becomes null; it does not store P or provide a tombstone → request → retained-owner lookup.

Each deletion read has one 25-second operation deadline shared by session-scope publication and the completion attempt, within the route’s 30-second `maxDuration`. Completion additionally has a 20-second ceiling; statement, idle-in-transaction and supported `transaction_timeout` are 20 seconds, and `lock_timeout` is 22 seconds (strictly greater than completion’s transaction timeout). `transaction_timeout` is set only when present in `pg_settings`. Failure, timeout, abort or deadlock rolls back completion and records a failed attempt in a separate write bounded to three seconds, leaving up to two seconds of route headroom. An operation/attempt deadline during completion returns the persisted queued receipt with `lastAttempt.outcome=failed` after that write succeeds (or the frozen receipt if another attempt completed); client aborts and other failures remain unavailable. Publication timeout cannot claim a committed request and fails unavailable. A later GET, POST, or sweep retries.

### Deletion lock order and polling

Every queued-request publication, completion attempt (including sweep), and failed-attempt write takes `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtextextended('home.account-deletion.customer.v1:' || customer_id,0))` before any request-row or credential lock. Session paths resolve that customer from the credential first; attempt paths read it from the request without locking. Scope is rechecked after acquisition, and a changed customer fails closed. Only deletion writers take this namespaced customer lock: ordinary action/order/customer writers retain their existing credential fencing and do not acquire it. In particular, concurrent first POSTs from different linked credentials cannot each hold a credential while waiting on the other's request insertion/completion. Their queued session-owner-key publication serializes before completion acquires its credential set. This is an attempt-local deletion lock, not a prohibition on independent actions while queued. Completed receipt recovery and expiry of already-retained evidence have no live customer identity to publish or fence.

GET and POST both use blocking acquisition of the same customer advisory and `FOR UPDATE` for a queued request. The publication transaction sets `lock_timeout='22s'` and `statement_timeout='25s'`, under the shared 25-second operation deadline; lock timeout remains strictly longer than completion’s 20-second transaction/application ceiling. No busy path skips publication of a newly discovered session owner key. If completion commits between the initial credential read and lock acquisition, the queued row is gone; the path rechecks the credential and recovers the completed request by tombstone request ID instead of returning 404. Publication therefore commits before a subsequent completion or observes an already-completed deletion; it cannot retrospectively add scope to a frozen receipt. The retry attempt also takes blocking customer/request locks under the remainder of the shared 25-second deadline and a 20-second attempt ceiling. Client aborts or sustained contention can still fail the request closed; these are not nonblocking receipt polls.

Deletion-first attempts discover a linked credential's legacy actions before that credential's GET/POST can acquire the customer lock, including actions on never-recorded wallets. A live legacy action blocks completion; a terminal one is pseudonymized before credentials are deleted. The deletion-first regression retires the recorded-wallet blocker before starting completion, so only the unpublished linked legacy action can keep the request queued.

Funding provider names come from the registered provider's manifest `displayName`, independent of whether its current offering is enabled. Only an unregistered provider ID fails the completion closed. Deferred customer/operator recording contains and reports all write failures, including `ACCOUNT_DELETED` and `AUTH_UNAVAILABLE`, inside the scheduled callback; these do not escape as unhandled rejections after a successful money action. The synchronous sign-in recorder (`bestEffortCustomerRecord`, used by both session and Base proof routes) reports and rethrows `AccountDeletedError` and `AccountDeletionAuthUnavailable` so the auth boundary denies sign-in; only other capture failures stay best-effort.

## Retention basis, anchor and field allowlist

The basis is **minimum financial records for money correctness, reconciliation and duplicate-success protection**. The single duration constant is `FINANCIAL_EVIDENCE_RETENTION_YEARS` in `shared/account/financial-retention.ts` (five years). `financialEvidenceExpiresAt(completedAt)` computes expiry in the application; SQL does not encode a separate duration. The anchor is completion, after every money item is provably terminal, not the request time or the original transaction time.

| customers P | random id; closed status; neutral completion first/last seen and backfill source; created/updated timestamps; retained_until | credentials, wallets, country, invite_code, merged identity |

Card events are deleted, not retained. Transaction-level uniqueness survives because card transactions move with their cards before C's card accounts are removed. Card issuance takes the credential advisory lock and tombstone check, validates the live credential-linked non-retained owner, then locks the card account before any issuance provider call. The existing transaction holds these locks across the provider call and recording, so deletion cannot complete in an issued-but-unrecorded window; provider latency may delay the attempt, whose timeout still rolls back and retries. Card write adoption fails closed for retained provider card IDs; event/transaction ingest checks live card ownership and drops retained or unlinked provider events. Retained funding orders refuse late provider observations and receipt claims, including revival of abandoned orders; provider-order lookup excludes retained owners. The update guards also reject retained owner subjects after a racing completion, without changing identity fields. Receipt matching rejects an erased or invalid destination before any RPC read.

Other stores are deleted only in C's scope: provider credentials/customer links (including legacy credential tuples), preferences, invite codes, email requests, support conversations and children, operator events, wallet balance snapshots/history (history children cascade), wallets and credentials. Before pseudonymizing legacy actions, completion collects both their `account_address` and the Base address element of their proven credential-wide owner keys, validates address formats, takes sorted `home.customer-wallet.v1:<chainId>:<lowercase-address>` advisory locks, then rechecks enrollment. Addresses now enrolled to another customer are excluded from cache cleanup rather than failing completion: those caches belong to the current customer. Both recorded-wallet and legacy-address DELETEs also require no other-customer wallet for the address. Wallet enrollment takes the same lock after its credential lock and before customer/credential/wallet row writes; completion orders customer deletion lock → request → sorted credentials → sorted addresses → rows. These fenced legacy addresses join recorded wallets in address-keyed cleanup, including historical snapshots on never-recorded wallets. Public history assets, global operator settings, webhook subscription secrets, and other customers' records are not deletion targets. Merged stubs must have no remaining credentials/wallets or completion fails closed. Cross-customer invitation attribution is removed only where it references C: matching `customers.invite_code` becomes null, and matching `operator_events.props.inviteCode` / `inviterCustomerId` keys are stripped independently. Other event fields, amounts, facts and unrelated attribution remain unchanged. Signup attribution holds `FOR SHARE` on the inviter’s customer row through its commit; completion’s `FOR UPDATE` on that row waits before cleanup, preventing a racing signup from restoring deleted inviter references. Signup after completion sees no inviter. Real-trigger tests verify both the permitted strip and rejection of other updates. The narrowly scoped trigger exemptions require C’s structural deletion-audit marker (and C’s still-owned invite code for code cleanup); they use no GUC/session flag.

## Tombstones and revocation

Configure `ACCOUNT_DELETION_TOMBSTONE_SECRET` through `server/config/env` and `.env.example`: a dedicated, stable key of at least 32 UTF-8 bytes. The digest is hex HMAC-SHA256 of `home.account-deletion.v1\0<accountProvider>\0<subject>`. Tombstones contain only digest, request id, completion/expiry timestamps, and `key_id` (the first eight hex characters of the key's SHA-256). They never store a plain subject, address, email or token.

Deletion is unavailable without this key. With a database configured, auth/resolution may proceed without a key only if the tombstone table is empty; otherwise they fail closed with `AUTH_UNAVAILABLE`. Fixture sessions without a database skip the lookup. Keep the key stable while tombstones exist; replacing it is not a supported rotation procedure. The key-id diagnostic is not a replacement-key recovery mechanism.

`authorizeSession` denies credentials tombstoned within `ACCOUNT_DELETION_REVOCATION_WINDOW_MS` (the existing seven-day owner-session lifetime) with private `401 ACCOUNT_DELETED`. Render seeds perform the same check and return no seed on revoked credentials or lookup failure. The customer resolver acquires the same digest lock and checks **before** inserting a credential. Action/funding/token/preference owner resolution must share the write transaction so completion cannot leave an identity-bearing legacy write behind. Deletion GET/POST use a separate raw cryptographically verified boundary solely to recover receipts; it issues no cookies and grants no other access. POST recovers a tombstone within the seven-day revocation window. Outside that window, both GET and POST first check for a live credential: its current customer always gets its own request state. Only when no live credential exists can either method recover the completed receipt from the tombstone until tombstone expiry, including after the seven-day window. The operator page path (`/admin`, through `readOperatorPageDecision`) is separately governed by the operator address allowlist and is exempt from customer tombstone checks; this does not exempt customer/private API authorization. Ordinary `/api/session` and Base proof verification check revocation before returning any session cookie or render hint, and await customer capture, whose real synchronous recorder rethrows both typed deletion-boundary errors, so typed revocation cannot become a successful sign-in; unrelated capture errors remain best-effort. Base proof verification clears its challenge cookie on both ACCOUNT_DELETED and AUTH_UNAVAILABLE, including denial during customer capture. After the window, fresh sign-in creates a new, empty customer; prior owner keys and funding subjects were rewritten.

## Limits

Action outcome reconciliation, like card event reconciliation, happens on owner reads. An abandoned transaction-hash action without an outcome can keep a deletion request queued until the owner opens Activity. Deletion attempts and sweep do not independently reconcile those outcomes.

The legacy registry backfill commands (`db:backfill-operator`, `db:backfill-customer-ids`) predate deletion and would recreate identity from pseudonymized or in-flight rows. Each command takes a share lock on `account_deletion_requests` and refuses to run while any request or retained pseudonymous owner exists (test: `legacy registry backfill commands refuse to run once a deletion request exists`). The migration runner's 011/017 backfills are unchanged and run before migration 024 creates the table.

## Expiry and operations

Run `bun run --cwd apps/web db:account-deletion-sweep` regularly. It processes a bounded least-recently-attempted queued batch (`last_attempt_at NULLS FIRST, id`), classifies queued attempts by `lastAttempt.outcome` (including deadline/timeouts as failed, not blocked), then expires retained owners/evidence and completed requests/tombstones. Default batch size is 50; the implementation accepts 1–500. Expiry comparisons include the exact `retained_until` boundary. Evidence removal is transactional: actions cascade to cash-out orders/fees; customers cascade to card accounts/cards/transactions. Expired requests cascade to tombstones. Partial batches are idempotent and reruns converge.

Audit deletion is separately authorized by `account_deletion_audit_expiry`: DELETE is allowed only for `target_kind='customer'`, an expiry row at or before database `now()`, and no live customer. There is no GUC/session bypass. UPDATE and TRUNCATE remain forbidden. Sweep deletes audit before its expiry marker.

## Stale balance observations

The balance reader can outlive its authorization check, including background revalidation. PostgreSQL snapshot writes now require a currently enrolled wallet on a non-retained customer and an observation time at or after that wallet's enrollment. The write transaction holds shared locks on both customer and wallet before touching the snapshot; completion holds an exclusive customer lock before deleting snapshots. A write therefore either finishes before completion (and is deleted) or observes no live wallet after completion and returns false. An old observation cannot cross later enrollment of the same address. Unknown/unenrolled addresses can still be read from the chain but are not persisted. The regression is `stale owner balance observation cannot recreate deleted snapshot or write across fresh wallet enrollment`.

Sweep covered an empty expiry set and a due bounded batch, including the request DELETE and tombstone FK trigger.

- Credential-wide legacy scope was additionally measured on PostgreSQL 14.24 with 20,000 synthetic customers and 40,000 actions (20,000 null-customer legacy rows), committed migrations, refreshed statistics and normal planner settings. The actual parameterized blocker SELECT used one exact owner key and two credential LIKE patterns. Without the new partial index it scanned all 40,000 actions (929 shared-hit buffers, 2 returned rows, 2.888 ms). With `actions_legacy_owner_pattern_idx`, a BitmapOr combines `actions_customer_created_idx` with partial pattern-index scans for exact/prefix keys (2 returned rows, 9 shared-hit buffers, 0.032 ms, no sort/spill). An empty-customer/empty-exact-key/missing-subject query uses the same selective indexes (0 rows, 9 shared-hit buffers, 0.015 ms). The fixed-prefix index condition narrows the legacy scan; the full LIKE predicate checks the Base/provider suffix. This resolves unknown-wallet discovery without scanning all legacy rows. The index is built transactionally by migration 024; production build duration, write amplification and lock impact still require rollout review.
- Actions use selective customer/owner indexes; cash-out counts join scoped actions to `cashout_orders_pkey`; pending card counts use `cards_customer_mode_idx` and `card_transactions_card_time_idx`. Explicit authorization closure is classified directly on the scoped card transaction without a matching-capture join; updated completion-lock, closure-classification and legacy-address cleanup representative plans remain rollout-review obligations. The new legacy-address selection uses the same indexed credential-wide action predicate; address conflict checks and cleanup use the existing chain/address indexes. Local lifecycle regressions establish scope and rollback correctness, not production plan performance.
- The initial funding predicate visited all 1,500 legacy rows (112 buffers, about 0.4 ms locally). It was replaced with a union of customer-index and credential-tuple-index scopes, followed by primary-key lookup. The revised query uses `funding_orders_customer_created_idx`, `customer_credentials_customer_idx`, `funding_orders_owner_open_idx` and `funding_orders_pkey` (11 buffers, about 0.04 ms). The same selective scope applies to provider-link cleanup/receipt reads; no additional funding index was needed.
- Tombstone point lookup uses `account_deletion_tombstones_pkey` (2 buffers for a miss). The earlier oldest-request plan used `account_deletion_queued_time_idx`; that partial index now orders `last_attempt_at NULLS FIRST,id` to prevent blocked batches starving later requests. The real PostgreSQL regression verifies progression past a full blocked batch. A second disposable PostgreSQL 14 plan loaded 1,000 queued rows (one third never attempted) and 5,000 completed rows with refreshed statistics: the revised bounded selection used an index-only scan on this partial index, no sort, 49 shared buffers and approximately 0.016 ms execution locally. Attribution and cardholder-cleanup representative plans still need rollout review. retained-owner/audit expiry selection uses the corresponding expiry indexes with `LockRows`/`SKIP LOCKED`. Completed-request expiry uses `account_deletion_expiry_idx` then the request primary key; the bounded DELETE, including the cascade trigger, was about 0.04 ms locally.

These are synthetic local plans, not provider/production performance proof. Broad owners, large due-expiry backlogs, production lock contention and index-build duration remain rollout review obligations. Expiry indexes include the deterministic tie-breaker (`retained_until,id`, `expires_at,id`, or `expires_at,target_id`); measured bounded-batch plans use ordered index scans without sorting the entire due set. No production database was changed.
